You can see the argument for Hotel Vancouver from three blocks away, before you've set foot inside: that green copper roof and its gargoyles rising over West Georgia Street like a châteauesque exclamation mark in the middle of a glassy modern downtown. As someone who reads buildings for a living, I find it thrilling that a 1939 railway hotel still holds its own against the towers around it. As a traveller, what I care about is whether the grandeur survives the threshold — and here, mostly, it does.

The lobby is the real thing: high, warm, genuinely grand, the kind of space that makes you stand up a little straighter. Check-in was fast and the front desk was warm and professional, a note guests repeat with striking consistency. And yes, I met the resident Labrador — the hotel's canine ambassador — which is exactly the sort of small, humane touch that turns a landmark into a favourite. More than one reviewer has said meeting the dog was a highlight of their stay, and I refuse to pretend I was above it.

Location is the headline

If you're weighing this hotel against Vancouver's newer luxury addresses, understand what you're buying: position. You can walk to Robson Street, the Art Gallery, the shopping and, with a little more effort, Stanley Park and the seawall. For a city trip where you'd rather not touch a car, it's close to unbeatable. The indoor lap pool, ringed with city windows, is a lovely place to start a morning, and afternoon tea is a proper occasion. Downstairs, Notch8 is a genuinely good room for dinner and cocktails — heritage setting, contemporary kitchen — and worth booking even if you're staying elsewhere.

You're buying a landmark and a location, not the last word in square footage — and on those terms it delivers handsomely.

The trade-offs of a heritage room

Here's where my architect's eye and my reviewer's honesty meet. A 1939 building was not designed around 2026 expectations of space, and the rooms are noticeably smaller than those in the glass towers a few streets away — a fair and frequent guest observation. The masonry that makes the exterior so grand doesn't always translate to soundproofing inside; several travellers, and I on one restless night, noticed thin walls and neighbouring-room noise. And while my room was comfortable, quiet and full of lovely fixtures, others feel a touch dated against the property's luxury billing. If a big, sound-sealed, modern box is what helps you sleep, this may not be your hotel.

Two practical notes. Valet parking is the norm here and it adds up over a stay, so factor it in if you're driving. And while service is generally excellent — staff fixed a small issue for me quickly and graciously — I did read the occasional account of a maintenance problem met with slow follow-up. My experience was the warm, attentive one; just know the other exists.

Who it's for

This is a hotel for the traveller who values character, a grand public realm and a location that walks you to everything, and who understands that heritage comes with cosier rooms and older walls. Ask for a higher, quieter floor, book Notch8, and say hello to the dog on your way out.

The verdict

A characterful downtown landmark with a grand lobby, warm service and a location that's hard to beat on foot. The heritage rooms run small and the walls are thinner than the marble suggests, so it won't suit everyone — but for a city stay steeped in atmosphere, with a Labrador in the lobby and Robson Street at the door, Hotel Vancouver remains a deeply likeable choice.
