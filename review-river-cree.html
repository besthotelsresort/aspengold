River Cree sits just past Edmonton's western edge, on Enoch Cree Nation land, and it is a genuinely unusual animal: a Marriott-managed resort casino with two NHL-sized ice rinks bolted to one side and a proper concert-and-comedy venue on the other. I arrived expecting a gaming barn with rooms attached and found something more considered — a modern hotel that mostly knows what it's doing, wrapped around an entertainment complex that keeps the place humming well past midnight.

The rooms are the easy win. Mine was modern, comfortable and quiet, with a bed I was reluctant to leave and towels soft enough to notice — a small thing that guests here mention again and again. The front desk was friendly and the security staff visibly helpful, which matters more at a 24-hour property than people expect. I'll single out one thing that too few resorts get right: the accessible rooms are excellent. A wheelchair-using guest called the roll-in shower "the best I've seen," and praised how quickly management responded when she needed something. That is not an accident; that is a property that has thought about who walks through its doors.

The venue, the rinks and the floor

As a former sound engineer, I judge a room by its acoustics, and The Venue holds up — an honest mid-size hall that treats touring acts and comedians fairly. The twin ice rinks give the place a rhythm you don't get elsewhere; on my visit there was a tournament on, and the lobby had the cheerful chaos of hockey bags and coffee cups at an hour when most hotels are silent. The casino floor is large, with around a thousand slots and thirty-odd tables, though I'll pass along a regular's grumble that the slots "feel tight." Take that for what it's worth from a man who tips the machines and expects nothing back.

The bones are strong and the beds are excellent — it's the kitchen and the small print that need attention.

Where the seams show

The most consistent disappointment here is food, and I can't sugar-coat it. The buffet and conference catering draw fair criticism — dishes served merely warm, the odd overcooked plate — and Tap 25, the sports-bar anchor, has slipped in the eyes of repeat visitors. The bright spot is smaller and humbler: the subs from Cree Subs are genuinely good, and I'd happily make a meal of one. If you're here for an event with catered meals, temper your expectations; if you're free to choose, choose carefully.

Two other themes deserve a mention because they keep surfacing. The first is billing and communication — a guest charged an early check-in fee after being told fees were waived, and others left unclear on overnight parking rules. The second is the occasional lapse in care: one traveller had small items go missing from a locked room, though I'll note the staff responded with an apology and replacement treats, which is the right instinct even if the incident shouldn't have happened. The pool, too, can get overcrowded and chaotic at peak times. None of these are dealbreakers on their own; together they're the difference between a solid stay and a seamless one.

Who it's for

River Cree is a smart, comfortable base if you're catching a show, chasing a tournament, or simply want a modern room a short shuttle from the airport without paying downtown attention to the drive. Come for the beds and the venue, keep your dining flexible, and read your folio at checkout.

The verdict

A comfortable, well-located resort casino with standout accessible rooms and a genuinely good live venue, let down chiefly by inconsistent food and a few avoidable slips in billing and communication. Sleep here is easy and the welcome is warm; the resort just needs to bring its kitchens and its small print up to the standard its beds already set. For a night near Edmonton with entertainment on tap, it's a fair bet — go in informed.
