Some hotels have to earn their location; the Château Laurier was simply born into the best one in Ottawa. This limestone-and-copper landmark sits where the Rideau Canal meets Parliament Hill, close enough to the seat of government that you half expect to pass a cabinet minister in the lift. I've reviewed a lot of grand railway hotels across this country, and few can match the sheer address of this one — step out the door and Parliament, the canal, the National Gallery and ByWard Market are all a short, pleasant walk away. As a base for seeing a capital city, it is almost unfairly convenient.

Inside, the good version of the Château Laurier is very good indeed. On my best morning here the reception was warm and quick, my room was spacious and spotless, and the whole building hummed with that easy, slightly formal atmosphere the great old hotels do so well. A guest ahead of me was singing the praises of a concierge named Samuel, and I understood why — when the service here is on, it makes you feel instantly at home. And then there's the pool: a genuine Art Deco jewel tucked below the lobby, all clean geometry and soft light, and the sort of surprise that makes a heritage hotel worth the stay.

When the Château Laurier is at its best, it feels like the beating heart of the capital; the trouble is that it isn't always at its best.

The honest caveat

I keep a notebook and a light meter with me for a reason, and this is a hotel where the details vary more than they should. The most consistent thread in recent guest feedback — and I saw the truth of it — is inconsistency from room to room. The photographs online flatter; some rooms are bright and generous, while others are small, dark and let down by thin walls. More than one guest has described tired maintenance where you'd least expect it: peeling paint, heavy dust, linens that didn't feel freshly laundered, a faint mustiness in an older room. In a national historic site you forgive a little age, but you shouldn't have to forgive a lack of upkeep.

Service, too, splits opinion. For every warm welcome like mine, there's a traveller who found the front desk dismissive or the valet slow and the check-in confusing. That unevenness is the hotel's real weakness — not the building, which is magnificent, but the follow-through, which depends too much on who happens to be on shift. My advice is practical: book directly, be specific about wanting a renovated, higher, quieter room, and don't be shy about asking to move if what you're given doesn't match the grandeur of the lobby you walked through.

The good life downstairs

Wilfrid's is a handsome room for breakfast or a proper dinner, and Zoe's Lounge is the right spot for a drink while you watch the capital drift past the windows. Add the spa and that Art Deco pool, and a rainy Ottawa afternoon takes care of itself without your ever leaving the building.

Who it's for

Choose the Château Laurier if location and heritage top your list and you're willing to advocate for a good room. Get the right one, catch the staff on a good day — which is more often than not — and you'll have the best seat in Ottawa.

The verdict

A magnificent, impeccably located landmark whose experience swings on the room you're given and the shift you catch. At its best — a bright renovated room, a warm welcome, that glorious pool — it's the finest address in the capital. At its worst it feels tired and impersonal. Book smart, ask for a refreshed room, and the odds tip firmly in your favour.
