There is a particular confidence to a building that has been receiving guests since 1912 and has no intention of stopping. The Ritz-Carlton on Sherbrooke Street — Canada's original Ritz, and the grande dame of Montréal's Golden Square Mile — carries that confidence lightly. I trained as an architect before I started writing about hotels, and I could have spent my whole stay just reading the Beaux-Arts detailing: the proportions of the lobby, the way the light is handled, the restraint of a renovation that modernised the rooms without embalming the history. It is, quite simply, one of the most assured hotels in the country.

Service is where it separates itself from merely beautiful competitors. My early check-in was granted without fuss, the bellman was so good he's become a minor legend in the guest reviews, and the front desk operated with the kind of unhurried precision that makes you feel like the only arrival that morning. This is a hotel where guests booking on points for an anniversary still find champagne and macarons waiting — a detail one couple mentioned with real delight — and where the staff, in the words of another regular, "take real pride." I saw it everywhere I looked.

The room, the pool and the table

My room blended old-world proportion with genuine comfort: heavy drapes, a serious bed, a bathroom that worked. I'll be straight with you, though — the standard rooms here are on the compact side, a common note in the reviews and a function of the building's age rather than any stinginess. If space matters to you, the corner suites (some with a working fireplace) are the ones worth chasing. Upstairs, the rooftop saltwater pool is a quiet triumph, a rare thing in a heritage hotel and a lovely place to lose an afternoon.

Downstairs, Maison Boulud is the reason half of Montréal seems to pass through the lobby, and dinner lived up to its billing. Afternoon tea in the Palm Court is the ritual to book — one guest celebrated a birthday there and singled out the "genuine warmth" of the staff, and I'd echo every word.

This is heritage handled by people who understand that grandeur is nothing without warmth — and here, both are in abundance.

Where even a grande dame slips

No hotel is flawless, and I'd be failing you if I pretended this one is. The most consistent complaint isn't about the bones but the follow-through: a few guests report a gap between the restaurant and in-room dining, and the occasional dated maintenance detail in the bathroom — the sort of small imperfection that stands out precisely because everything around it is so polished. More troubling to me were two service misfires that soured otherwise special stays: a requested sheet change that never arrived on an anniversary, and a guest who felt conspicuously over-watched by security while dressed casually. At this level, and at this price of expectation, those are the moments a hotel simply cannot afford, and they're worth naming plainly.

To be fair to the Ritz, these are the exceptions in a sea of five-star reviews, not the rule. But consistency is the last mountain for a hotel this good, and it's the one thing standing between an excellent stay and a perfect one.

Who it's for

Come here for an occasion — an anniversary, a celebration, a trip where the hotel is meant to be part of the memory rather than a place to sleep. Ask for a corner suite if you can, book the tea, and let the staff do what they do better than almost anyone in Canada.

The verdict

A restored heritage landmark that pairs Beaux-Arts beauty with warmth and precision most luxury hotels only aspire to. The standard rooms are snug and a handful of service slips keep it from a spotless record, but the overwhelming impression is of a grande dame in exceptional form. If you want to feel genuinely looked after in the heart of Montréal, few addresses come close.
